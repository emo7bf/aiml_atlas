First Constellation · The Linked List

Reverse a linked list — by hand, pointer by pointer.

Five stars, each knowing only its next. To reverse the chain, we re-aim one arrow at a time. Press ⟲ flip next pointer — slowly — and narrate what each flip does.
prev = None · curr = A
Every flip is the same 3-line dance: remember next → aim curr backward → walk forward. 
def reverse(head):
    prev = None                          # the already-reversed part (behind you)
    curr = head
    while curr:
        next_node  = curr.next           # ① remember the road ahead
        curr.next  = prev                # ② the flip you just clicked
        prev, curr = curr, next_node     # ③ walk forward one star
    return prev                          # prev ends as the new head · O(n)/O(1)
The three-pointer dance (prev/curr/next_node) is THE linked-list interview answer; forgetting to save next_node before the flip is the classic bug (you orphan the rest of the chain). Variants to mention: reverse between positions m..n, reverse in k-groups — same dance with bookkeeping. Lists vs arrays: O(1) insert/delete given the node, O(n) access — opposite trade of arrays.
Second Constellation · The Tree

Now you fly the traversal: tap the stars in BFS order.

One root ✦, no cycles, one path to every star. BFS explores level by level — ripples, not dives. Your turn to be the algorithm: tap the stars in BFS visit order, root first. (Within a level, left to right.)
tap the root when ready…
The machinery under your taps is a queue (FIFO): visit a star → enqueue its children → always serve the oldest waiting star. 
from collections import deque
def bfs(root):
    queue = deque([root])                # the ripple front
    while queue:
        star = queue.popleft()           # oldest waiting star (FIFO!)
        visit(star)
        queue.extend(star.children)      # next ripple joins the back
Expected tap order A,B,C,D,E,F,G (level order, left→right). Teal glow + numbered badge on correct, red shake on wrong — kinesthetic retrieval practice. Vocabulary to drop while tapping: root, leaf, depth/level, height; BFS finds shortest paths in UNWEIGHTED graphs (the #1 reason to choose it); swap the deque for a plain stack/pop() and the identical code becomes DFS — next slide.
Two Ways to Wander

Same sky, two temperaments: the diver and the rippler.

Watch them wander. DFS dives (a whole branch, then backtrack); BFS ripples (level by level). Play both — notice where each is at step 4.
The whole choice, one breath: BFS = nearest/shortest anything (unweighted) — it visits in distance order. DFS = paths, subtrees, exhaustive exploration — and recursion is DFS: the call stack is the stack.

DFS preorder here: A,B,D,E,C,F,G; BFS: A,B,C,D,E,F,G — at step 4 DFS is deep at E while BFS is still wide at D: that snapshot IS the difference. Bridge to the DP series: tree DP (Part 3 preview) runs on DFS post-order — children's answers before the parent's, which is just tabulation order on a tree. Both are O(V+E) time.
