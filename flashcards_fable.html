F01 ⋆Great offline, bad in the wildWHITE

MEANING · The debugging ladder when validation says yes but production says no. Walk it IN ORDER — each rung is cheaper to check than the next: ① leakage audit ② split shape vs deployment ③ training/serving skew ④ distribution shift ⑤ feedback loops ⑥ calibration/prevalence mismatch.
NOTATION · L̂val ≪ R(θ) under P_deploy — the gap between your estimate and the true deployment risk is the whole story.
GO · Rung ② is where cross-validation gets interrogated: if your CV is random folds but deployment predicts the future or new users, the CV estimate was answering a different question than the one production asked.
🤫 IYKYK · When CV score ≫ prod score, suspect the folds before the model: duplicate/near-duplicate rows straddling folds, same user in train and val, time travel. The model is usually innocent; the evaluation lied.

F02 ⋆Temporal & grouped cross-validationWHITE

MEANING · Match fold geometry to deployment geometry. Predicting forward in time → rolling-origin splits (train on past, validate on the next window). Entities that recur → GroupKFold so a user/document/patient never straddles folds.
NOTATION · train: t ≤ T₀ · val: T₀ < t ≤ T₁ — rolled forward k times.
STOP · Shuffling time-series data for CV is leakage with a respectable name.
🤫 IYKYK · Preprocessing must live INSIDE each fold (sklearn Pipeline inside cross_val_score). A scaler fit on the full dataset before splitting quietly peeked at the validation folds — the single most common leak in real notebooks.
→008, 017

F03Training/serving skewWHITE

MEANING · One feature, two implementations: computed by batch SQL for training, by a streaming service for serving. Subtle differences (window edges, timezone, null handling) silently degrade the live model while every offline metric stays green.
GO · One feature definition, two serving paths — that's the feature store's entire reason to exist.
🤫 IYKYK · The pro diagnostic: log raw serving-time inputs, replay them through the training pipeline, and diff the feature values. Any nonzero diff is a bug you can now point at.
→005, 017 · your 🧰 Infra deck, slide 7

F04Baseline-first disciplineWHITE

MEANING · Before any neural net: ① predict the majority class / mean (the floor) ② logistic or linear regression ③ gradient-boosted trees. Each step must EARN its complexity over the last.
GO · "I'd start with logistic regression" is the most Google-sounding sentence available in applied-ML interviews.
🤫 IYKYK · A baseline is also an instrument: if logistic ≈ your deep model, the features are the ceiling, not the architecture — go do feature work. If the majority-class baseline is already at 94%, your problem statement (or metric) needs renegotiating before any modeling.
→014, 020, 028, 029

F05PR-AUC vs ROC-AUC under imbalanceWHITE

MEANING · ROC's x-axis (FPR = FP/(FP+TN)) has the huge negative class in its denominator — so at 1% prevalence, oceans of false positives barely move it. Precision has predicted positives in the denominator and feels every one.
NOTATION · random PR-AUC ≈ prevalence, not 0.5 — a 0.3 PR-AUC at 1% prevalence is a 30× lift.
STOP · An impressive ROC-AUC on a rare-positive problem is the classic way to accidentally oversell a model.
🤫 IYKYK · Always report prevalence next to any AUC. Reviewers who ask "what's the base rate?" are doing exactly this check on you.
→018, 025, 026

F06Threshold economicsWHITE

MEANING · 0.5 is a default, not a decision. The threshold comes from the cost matrix or the capacity constraint: minimize expected cost, or take top-k where k = how many cases the fraud/retention team can actually work.
NOTATION · τ* = argmin_τ E[cost] = c_FP·FP(τ) + c_FN·FN(τ)
GO · Changing τ re-aims a trained model for free — say this before anyone suggests retraining.
🤫 IYKYK · When the constraint is team capacity, there is no threshold at all — it's a daily top-k, and your real metric is precision@k. Interviewers light up when a candidate converts a threshold question into a capacity question unprompted.
→024, 021, 153

F07Calibration in the wildWHITE

MEANING · A model is calibrated when "0.8" means 80%. Check with a reliability diagram (bin predictions, plot observed frequency); repair with Platt scaling (sigmoid fit) or isotonic regression on a held-out set.
STOP · Class weighting and resampling (card 018's remedies!) silently DESTROY calibration — the model now lives in a world with fake prevalence.
🤫 IYKYK · The order of operations pros use: train with weights if you must → recalibrate on untouched data at true prevalence → THEN pick the threshold. Skipping the middle step is why "the probabilities look weird" tickets exist.
→018, 027

F08Is the improvement real?WHITE

MEANING · Deep-learning runs are stochastic (init, shuffling, dropout, GPU nondeterminism). A single-run +0.5% is weather, not climate. Rerun with ≥3 seeds; report mean ± std; compare on the SAME folds (paired, not pooled).
NOTATION · Δ = mean(A−B over paired folds) — pairing removes fold difficulty as noise.
🤫 IYKYK · A +0.3% that survives five seeds beats a +1% that doesn't survive two. Teams that don't seed-sweep spend quarters chasing improvements that were never there.
→008, 013

F09Drift monitoring & late labelsWHITE

MEANING · Ground truth often arrives weeks late (fraud confirmations, churn windows). Until it does, monitor what you CAN see: input feature distributions (PSI/KL vs training), prediction distribution, and calibration drift.
NOTATION · PSI = Σ (pᵢ−qᵢ)·ln(pᵢ/qᵢ) — rule of thumb: >0.2 = investigate.
STOP · Data drift (inputs moved) ≠ concept drift (the input→label relationship moved). Input monitoring is blind to the second.
🤫 IYKYK · "Deploy is the midpoint, not the finish line — who notices when the model goes quietly wrong?" is a design-interview sentence that does a lot of lifting.
→040 · 🧰 Infra deck, slide 8

F10Feedback loopsWHITE

MEANING · Your model's decisions shape tomorrow's training data: the recommender only shows what it already likes; the fraud model only gets labels for cases it flagged. Retraining then amplifies yesterday's beliefs.
GO · Log the treatment (who got flagged/recommended, with what propensity) alongside the outcome — future-you needs it to de-bias.
🤫 IYKYK · Keep a small randomized holdout slice that never gets the model's intervention — it's simultaneously your unbiased label source AND your ongoing A/B control. Cheap insurance almost nobody buys until burned.
→040, 151

F11Slice-based evaluationWHITE

MEANING · Aggregate metrics average away your worst failures. Evaluate per segment — tenure, geography, device, class, input length — and gate deployment on "no slice regresses," not just "average improves."
STOP · A model better on average and worse for one user segment is a launch blocker at Google, full stop (fairness + long tail).
🤫 IYKYK · The slices that bite are the ones correlated with data volume: your model is worst exactly where you had least data, which is often exactly the market you're trying to grow into.
→020, 143

F12The prediction-time interrogationWHITE

MEANING · Card 017's question — "could I know this at the exact moment of prediction?" — asked like a prosecutor. The sneaky guilty parties: aggregates computed over the FULL dataset (including the future), fields edited after the outcome, join keys that only exist post-hoc, "days since last activity" computed at label time.
🤫 IYKYK · The tell in the wild: one feature with suspiciously huge importance. Feature-importance plots are leakage detectors first and interpretability tools second. If AUC ≈ 0.99 on a human-hard problem, you didn't win — you leaked.
→017, 005

F13Error analysis by handWHITE

MEANING · Before touching the architecture: pull 50 misclassified examples, read them, tag each with a failure reason (label wrong / ambiguous / feature missing / genuinely hard / preprocessing bug). The tag histogram IS your roadmap.
GO · If 20 of 50 errors are label noise, the winning move is relabeling, not a bigger model — data-centric beats model-centric embarrassingly often.
🤫 IYKYK · Andrew Ng made "look at your errors" a movement, but the quiet part: do it BEFORE the first model too (read 50 raw examples) — half of all pipeline bugs are visible to the naked eye.
→013, 014

F14 ⋆Read the constraints firstYELLOW

MEANING · The constraints whisper the intended solution: n ≤ 20 → bitmask/brute force (2ⁿ fine) · n ≤ ~1000 → O(n²) DP fine · n ≤ 10⁵ → need O(n log n) · budget/amount small → pseudo-polynomial DP · values up to 10¹⁸ → think math, not iteration.
GO · Say it aloud: "n is 10⁵, so I'm looking for n log n or better — that suggests sorting, heaps, or binary search."
🤫 IYKYK · This works in reverse for NP-hard recognition: if the clean formulations are all exponential and n ≤ 20, the SETTER chose 20 because they expect the 2ⁿ solution. The constraint is the permission slip.
→076, 094, 095

F15 ⋆Amortized O(1) in the wildYELLOW

MEANING · Python's list.append is amortized O(1): it over-allocates and doubles, so occasional O(n) copies average out. Dict/set inserts: same story via resize. That's card 078's "amortized" made concrete.
NOTATION · Σ cost of n appends = O(n) even though one append can cost O(n).
STOP · heapify is O(n), but n heappushes are O(n log n) — building a heap the "obvious" way forfeits the discount.
🤫 IYKYK · list.pop(0) is O(n) and "string" += in a loop is O(n²) — the two Python performance bugs interviewers can spot from across the room. deque and "".join are the fixes.
→078, 111, 118, 121, 123

F16 ⋆Top-k decision ladderGREEN

MEANING · Need the k best of n? Full sort O(n log n) when you need ALL in order · heap O(n log k) when k ≪ n or data streams · quickselect O(n) average when you need the k-th (unordered) in memory.
GO · "k is small and the data streams, so a size-k min-heap" — picking BY REGIME is the senior move, same instinct as your serving-engine table.
🤫 IYKYK · heapq.nlargest(k, it) already implements the ladder's heap rung — and for k=1 it quietly degrades to max(). Using stdlib then stating the complexity beats hand-rolling it slightly wrong.
→119, 120, 082

F17 ⋆Monotonic stackGREEN

MEANING · A stack you keep sorted by popping everything your new element beats. Solves the whole "nearest greater/smaller to the left/right" family in O(n): next greater element, daily temperatures, largest rectangle in histogram, trapping rain water.
NOTATION · while stack and a[stack[-1]] < a[i]: resolve(stack.pop()) — each index pushed and popped at most once ⇒ O(n).
🤫 IYKYK · The trigger phrases: "next greater/smaller," "span until a bigger one," "visible from the right." Hear any of them → monotonic stack, and say the push-once-pop-once argument for O(n) out loud — it's the part that impresses.
→113, 078

F18Two pointers & sliding windowGREEN

MEANING · Sorted array + pair/sum condition → two pointers from the ends, O(n). Contiguous subarray + "longest/shortest/count with property" → sliding window: grow right, shrink left while invalid, O(n).
STOP · Sliding window needs monotone feasibility (growing the window can't FIX a violation) — with negatives in a sum problem, that breaks, and you're back to prefix sums + hashmap.
🤫 IYKYK · The window-invariant sentence to narrate: "the window is always the largest valid window ending at right." State it, and your while-loop writes itself — and reviewers hear a loop invariant, which is card 074 energy.
→074, 086, 121

F19Embedding-space sanity checksORANGE

MEANING · Before trusting any system built on embeddings (retrieval, RecSys, RAG), interrogate the space itself: nearest neighbors of known items should be sensible; distances should separate obvious pairs; a 2-D projection (UMAP/t-SNE) should show structure, not soup.
GO · Ten manual nearest-neighbor queries catch more embedding bugs than any aggregate metric — wrong pooling, unnormalized vectors, truncated text all show up instantly.
🤫 IYKYK · Cosine vs dot product is a real decision: normalized embeddings make them equivalent; unnormalized dot product smuggles in popularity (longer vectors win) — sometimes you WANT that (RecSys), sometimes it's a bug (semantic search). Know which one your index uses.

F20Offline ↔ online metric mappingORANGE

MEANING · Each stage gets its own offline metric (retrieval → recall@k; ranking → NDCG/MRR; classifier → PR-AUC), but the business decides on online metrics (CTR, conversion, retention) via A/B test. Offline gates candidates; online crowns them.
STOP · Offline lifts routinely evaporate online: position bias, feedback loops, and distribution shift between logged data and live traffic (cards F10 + 040 conspiring).
🤫 IYKYK · "We'd validate with an A/B before trusting the offline lift" is the closing sentence of every strong applied-ML answer. For ranking changes specifically, name interleaving — more sensitive than A/B at the same traffic, and a true insider word.
